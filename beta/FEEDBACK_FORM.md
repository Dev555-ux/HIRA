# HIRA Beta Feedback Form

Paste these directly into Google Forms / Typeform / Tally. Keep it to one sitting — long forms get abandoned or rushed. Every open-ended question below is deliberately not yes/no; if a tester answers in one word, follow up with them directly rather than editing the question to force more detail on the next round (that's what erodes signal quality).

## Open-ended (free text)

1. **What was the first thing you tried?** (Not what we told you to try — what did you actually click first?)
2. **What confused you?** Be specific — where were you, and what did you expect to happen instead?
3. **What impressed you?**
4. **What feature did you expect to find but couldn't?**
5. **Describe a real situation from your business where you'd actually use this.** (If nothing comes to mind, say that instead — it's useful information too.)
6. **If HIRA disappeared tomorrow, what would you miss?** (If the honest answer is "nothing," say that. We'd rather know now.)
7. **What's one thing you'd change before recommending this to another business owner?**
8. **Anything else?** (Open catch-all — don't skip this one; it's where the most surprising answers tend to land.)

## Ratings (1–10, 10 = best)

Ask for a number *and* leave a comment field under each — the number alone can't tell you *why* a 6 is a 6.

9. **Ease of use**: 1–10, + "what would move this up a point?"
10. **AI quality** (did it understand you, ground answers in your actual business, avoid making things up): 1–10, + "give an example of a good or bad answer"
11. **Speed**: 1–10, + "was there a specific moment it felt slow?"
12. **Overall experience**: 1–10, + "one sentence on why that number and not higher"

## One closing question, asked exactly this way

13. **How would you feel if you could no longer use HIRA?**
    - Very disappointed
    - Somewhat disappointed
    - Not disappointed
    - (This is the Sean Ellis PMF test — don't reword it, and don't explain the rating scale on ease-of-use/speed etc. right before asking it. It needs to be answered cold.)

## Form-design notes

- Keep it under 10 minutes. If it's longer, testers rush the last third and you lose exactly the reflective answers you wanted.
- Don't make every field required — a skipped question is better than a fabricated answer to satisfy a required field.
- Send it the same day they test, not a week later. Memory of specific confusion fades fast; the specifics are the whole value of open-ended questions.
