# HIRA — THE EXPERIENCE FILM

**Type:** Internal creative direction. The north star.
**Status:** Final creative artifact before implementation.
**Reads alongside:** `DESIGN-SCREENBOOK-001` (layout, locked), `DESIGN-MOTION-001` (motion system, approved).
**How to use this document:** This is not a spec and not marketing. It is the *felt* experience, written as a film so that every engineer, designer, and product decision has one thing to compare against. When a choice is ambiguous, the question is not "what does the ticket say" — it is **"is this consistent with the film?"** If a build feels right on paper but wrong against this document, the document wins until we consciously decide otherwise.

---

## DIRECTOR'S NOTE (read before the scenes)

This film has no soundtrack swell, no hero shot of a product with a lens flare, no voice saying "imagine a world." That is deliberate. HIRA is not exciting the way a launch is exciting. HIRA is the feeling of a good hire on their third week — when you stop checking their work because it keeps being right.

So the direction is **quiet on purpose.** The camera is patient. Nothing rushes to impress. The most important moments in this film are moments of *stillness* — because stillness is what competence looks like. An anxious product fidgets. A confident one waits.

Five words govern every frame. If a moment doesn't serve at least one, we cut it:

> **Confidence. Competence. Trust. Calm. Precision.**

We never say "AI." We never say "magic." We never say "powered by." The founder does not care that it is intelligent — they care that the work is done. So we show the work, honestly, and let the intelligence be *inferred* the way you infer it from a colleague: not from a glow, but from a good decision made without you.

A note on the recurring visual motif, because it carries the whole film: **the working line.** It is a single thin line — foreground-toned, no glow, no color — that stays flat and still when nothing is happening, and carries a small travelling pulse along its length when work is flowing through. It is the same line whether the AI is thinking, drafting, booking, or handing something to the founder; it just moves differently. Treat it as the product's body language. Watch for it in every scene.

## SCENE 1 — LANDING

### *The founder arrives.*

**FADE IN.**

**EXT. — A LAPTOP, LATE EVENING.** We do not see the founder's face. We see the screen, and we see it the way they see it: leaning in slightly, a little tired, mildly skeptical. They have tried tools before. They typed the URL themselves. This is not a warm lead — this is a busy person giving something ninety seconds.

The page is already there when it loads. No spinner. No white flash — even though it's dark out and their OS is in dark mode, the first paint is *dark*, correct, settled. **(There is no theme flicker, ever. The room is the right color before the founder's eyes focus.)**

**CAMERA: slow push-in on the fold.**

The first impression is *space.* Not emptiness — space. The layout breathes. There is a headline, a single line of support beneath it, and one primary action. Nothing else competes.

**THE HERO REVEAL — one composed breath.**

The headline, the supporting line, and the primary button rise together — a four-pixel lift, a soft fade, decelerating as they arrive, the way a hand sets an object down and lets go. They are staggered by a breath — forty milliseconds — leader, then support, then the call to action, so the eye is *led* through them in reading order. The whole entrance is over in under three hundred milliseconds. Fast. Confident. Done.

It does not animate for two seconds while the founder waits. A product that makes you watch it introduce itself does not respect your time, and HIRA's entire premise is that it respects your time.

**LIGHTING & DEPTH.**

There is one light source in this world, and it never moves: soft, faintly cool, from above and just left of center. Every shadow in the entire product obeys it. Here it means the primary button sits *just slightly* above the page — a low, soft, downward shadow, not a hard edge, not a glow. In dark mode there is no black shadow (it would vanish); instead the button's top edge catches a single pixel of light, the way a raised surface does. The founder cannot name this. They only register that the surface feels like a real material — matte, like good paper — and not like a website.

There is no glass. No neon. No gradient wash of brand color. No particles drifting. The premium feeling is made entirely of *restraint and light.*

**TYPOGRAPHY.**

The numbers, if any are visible, are tabular — the digits sit in even columns. The headline is balanced so no word hangs alone on the last line. Quotes are real quotes; dashes are real dashes. None of this is conscious. All of it is felt as "this was made by someone who cared."

**CURSOR BEHAVIOUR.**

As the founder moves the pointer, it *means* something everywhere. Over the button: a pointer, and the button warms — a brightness shift over a hundred-and-twenty milliseconds, no movement, because controls don't leap. Over text they might want to read: a text cursor. Over empty space: nothing pretends to be clickable. The interface never lies about what it is.

**SCROLL BEHAVIOUR.**

They scroll. The page moves exactly with their fingers — one to one, native momentum intact. **Nothing is scroll-jacked.** No section grabs the scroll and holds it hostage for a "cinematic sequence." HIRA would never take the wheel from you; that is the whole relationship.

As they scroll, each section is at rest until it enters the frame, then settles in with the same small rise-and-fade — **once.** Scroll back up and nothing re-performs. A thing that re-animates every time you pass it is a thing performing for you, and HIRA does not perform.

**THE MOMENT THAT LANDS.**

Midway down, they reach it — the one place the page comes alive. A small arrangement of the business's systems, each doing its own quiet work, independent, unhurried. The founder watches for a second. Then — in a single instant, all at once, genuinely simultaneous — every system settles into a shared resting state, one soft brightness breath across the whole row, seven hundred milliseconds, and then still again. Along the connections between them, a thin pulse travels: work, moving through a business.

This is the only "big" motion on the page. It is not a trick. It is a **demonstration**: *this is what it looks like when your business is running and you are not touching it.* The founder doesn't consciously decode that. They just feel the thing the entire product is promising, before they've read a word of how it works.

**CAMERA: hold on the founder's stillness.** They have stopped scrolling. Ninety seconds became four minutes. They didn't decide to keep going; they just didn't leave.

## SCENE 2 — WATCHING THE RECEPTIONIST

### *A real message arrives. The AI notices. We watch it think.*

**INT. — THE PRODUCT, INBOX.** The founder is in now. They've connected WhatsApp. They are looking at a quiet inbox, and — because we are honest — nothing is happening. That stillness is not dead air. It is the interface at ease. An idle employee is not anxious.

**CAMERA: locked off, wide on the inbox. We wait with them.**

Then, a real message arrives.

**THE MESSAGE LANDS.** It enters on the *human* side of the surface — plain, unstyled, no product treatment, because it came from a person and we never dress a person up as software. A small unread weight settles beside it. A customer said: *"Hi, do you have anything Saturday morning? Need a cut and beard trim."*

**Beat.** The founder's instinct — the old instinct from every other tool — is *now I have to do something.* But the camera holds, because HIRA has already started.

**THE AI NOTICES.**

Beneath the message, on the AI's side of the surface — a distinct material, faintly set apart by a quiet spine down its left edge, so there is never any doubt about who is speaking — **the working line appears, and begins to move.** A small pulse travels along it. Unhurried. This is *thinking.* Not three bouncing dots borrowed from a chat app. A line, working, the same visual language as the systems on the landing page — because it is the same entity, and it behaves consistently everywhere.

**KNOWLEDGE ACTIVATES — and this is the most important transition in the film.**

Before any answer forms, something arrives *first*: a small chip settles onto the AI surface. *Saturday hours: 9am–2pm.* Then another: *Cut + beard — 45 min.* The **evidence lands before the conclusion.**

This ordering is not decorative and it is not negotiable. Watching the receptionist *fetch what it knows* before it speaks is the entire difference between "it looked this up" and "it made this up." A sighted founder sees the chips land. A founder using a screen reader hears the same thing announced, in the same order. Both watch it reason. Neither is asked to take intelligence on faith — they are *shown the receipts.*

**REASONING UNFOLDS — in sequence, because sequence is what thought looks like.**

If the founder wants to see the working, they expand it. The reasoning reveals itself one step at a time — each step settling in after the last, over a few hundred milliseconds total. Even if the system computed all of it at once, we reveal it *in order*, because a wall of reasoning appearing simultaneously reads as a data dump, and a chain revealing link by link reads as a mind. The founder can collapse it instantly. Most times, after the first few, they won't bother expanding it at all — which is the goal. You stop checking a good hire's work.

**A DRAFT APPEARS.**

The line completes its pass and comes to rest, and the reply resolves onto the AI surface:

> *"Hi! Yes — we've got Saturday morning open. I can do a cut and beard trim together, about 45 minutes. Would 10:30 work? Happy to find another time if not."*

It did not stream character by character to look busy. If it was ready, it is simply *there*, complete, correct, grounded in the two facts we watched arrive. Nothing about this was faked. There was no artificial "thinking" delay staged to seem clever — if the work had been instant, the line would have barely appeared, and that speed would have been its own kind of reassurance.

**THE FEELING.**

The founder did not write this. They did not look anything up. They watched a competent colleague read a customer, check the calendar and the service list, and draft exactly what they themselves would have written — only faster, and while they were pouring a coffee.

**CAMERA: slow push toward the draft, now at rest, waiting.** Because it *is* waiting. And that is the next scene.

## SCENE 3 — APPROVING

### *The founder presses one button. Everything downstream connects.*

**INT. — INBOX, CONTINUOUS.** The draft sits at rest. Below it, the review controls have arrived and **come to a stop** — Approve, Edit, Reject, Escalate. The stillness of that stopped line is a promise the founder can feel without reading it: *nothing goes out without you.* This is Assisted mode. The gate is closed. Every draft flows up to this line and waits.

The founder reads the reply. It's right. Their pointer moves to **Approve.**

**THE PRESS.**

On press-down, the button gives — a scale to ninety-eight percent, seventy-five milliseconds, immediate. This is the single most important tactile moment in the entire product, and it must have zero perceptible latency, because latency *here* is the difference between a tool that feels alive under your hand and one that feels like it's buffering your intentions.

On release, the button settles back with the faintest overshoot — a spring, capped so small it reads as *life*, never as a toy bouncing. A single soft brightness breath confirms: *committed.*

**THE DISPATCH — direction carries meaning.**

The draft **lifts and departs outward** — off the edge of the card, toward the world, toward the customer. Outward always means "sent to a customer" in this product; the founder will learn to read that direction peripherally, without labels, the way you learn a colleague's shorthand. The card completes and leaves. The queue closes the gap in a smooth reflow — no hard jump — and the next item, if there is one, settles up into focus.

It should feel like **clearing your desk.** One confident motion, and the thing is handled and gone.

**AND THEN — everything connects.**

The founder didn't navigate anywhere. But the product is one connected organism, and they can feel it move as one:

- The customer, on their phone, receives the message. (We trust this happened; we don't need to prove it on screen — but the founder knows the send was real, because HIRA is optimistic about the founder's action and would visibly *take it back*, with an explanation, if the send had failed. It didn't.)
- The customer replies *"10:30 is perfect."* The receptionist, still working, moves to **book.** The working line travels toward the calendar, and on Saturday at 10:30 a slot **fills in** — it solidifies into place rather than blinking into existence, marked subtly as work the AI did, not work a human typed.
- On the dashboard — which the founder isn't even looking at — a number has quietly become true. When they next glance at it, the appointment count will have *rolled* up by one, digit over digit, not silently swapped. Change, in HIRA, is always *witnessed.*

**THE EMOTION.**

Confidence. Not the jolt of a notification, not the little dopamine ping of a "success!" toast with confetti. Confetti would be HIRA congratulating *itself*, and HIRA never does that. The success here is *quiet* — as quiet as success is when a good employee does the obvious right thing. The founder approved one message and a customer got booked into their Saturday. The whole chain moved together, and none of it felt like software. It felt like saying "yes, send that" to someone across the room, and knowing it's handled.

**CUT TO:**

## SCENE 4 — THE DASHBOARD

### *Walking into an office where work is already underway.*

**INT. — OVERVIEW.** The founder opens the dashboard. The direction here is precise and it is a rule: **opening the dashboard must feel like walking into a running office, not like booting a program.**

**THE SHELL IS ALREADY THERE.** The sidebar and the top bar do not animate in. They are the walls and the door frame — already present, already true, every single time. Re-animating the furniture on every visit would punish the founder for using the product often, and HIRA is built to be used often. Only the *content* — the work on the desks — arrives.

**THE ARRIVAL.**

The content region resolves. On a first load, its shape is there before its data is — quiet placeholders that match the final layout *exactly*, so that when the real numbers arrive, nothing shifts, nothing jumps, nothing reflows. The placeholders cross-fade to real data. The greeting settles first, then the KPIs, then the panels, in a tight, capped sequence. Settled in well under half a second. On every visit after, cached data is simply *there* — instant — and any change reconciles quietly underneath. **HIRA never makes you re-watch a loading state for data it already has.**

**THE KPIs — witnessed, not decorated.**

A number that *changed* since last time rolls to its new value — odometer-like, over a hundred-and-twenty milliseconds, digits in their even tabular columns so nothing jitters. A number that *did not change* **does not move at all.** This restraint is the whole point. A dashboard where everything pulses and shimmers on every load is a dashboard crying wolf; you learn to ignore it. In HIRA, motion is scarce enough that *when something moves, it means something changed* — so the founder can read the health of the business from motion alone, at a glance, from across the room.

There is no count-up-from-zero animation. That would be a small lie — for half a second the number on screen would not be the true number — and HIRA does not lie, not even for a flourish.

**THE RECEPTIONIST, QUIETLY WORKING.**

There is a view of the employee's state, and it uses the same body language as everywhere else. Right now: **idle. Perfectly still.** And this is a design decision that takes nerve — the still state is *completely still.* No ambient shimmer, no breathing glow, no fidget to prove it's "alive." Because a person at their desk, available, nothing wrong, is *still* — and a state that fidgets while idle reads as anxious, or broken. HIRA's confidence is expressed as much by how comfortable it is doing nothing as by how it moves when there's work.

When a customer messages, *that* is when the founder will see the line begin to travel — and because the rest of the dashboard is so calm, that small motion in the corner of the eye will read, unmistakably, as *"something just came in, and it's already being handled."* The arrival is the reassurance.

**RECENT ACTIVITY.**

A feed, filling from the top at reading pace. New entries enter with the small rise-and-fade; existing ones slide down gently to make room — never a hard shove. It flows in like a calm colleague mentioning what they've done, not like a firehose. Nothing here demands the founder's attention. Nothing here is *trying.* It simply keeps an honest, quiet record of a business running.

**CAMERA: pull back slowly. The whole dashboard, mostly still, one small line working in the corner.** This is the thesis of the product in a single frame: calm surface, quiet competence underneath.

## SCENE 5 — INBOX

### *Where the promise is felt, message by message. Every action means something.*

**INT. — INBOX, THE FOUNDER SETTLES IN.** This is where the founder will spend real time, and so this is where every interaction has to *earn its weight.* We walk through the vocabulary of actions the way a director blocks a scene — each one distinct, each one meaningful.

**READING.** The founder opens a conversation. The customer's words are on the human surface, calm, fully selectable — they can copy anything. The interface does not move while they read. Reading is sacred; nothing animates under the founder's eyes while they're taking something in.

**THINKING & REASONING.** For a fresh message, the working line travels, the grounding chips land first, the reply builds — exactly the honest sequence from Scene 2. For messages already drafted, the work is simply done and waiting. The reasoning is collapsed by default — calm — and unfolds step by step only if the founder asks to see it. The product's default posture is *"I've handled the thinking; look only if you want to."*

**APPROVING.** As in Scene 3 — press, spring, brightness breath, the reply departs outward, the desk clears, the next item rises. Rhythm sets in. Approve, approve, approve. The founder falls into a cadence, and the cadence *feels good* — a small, repeated confirmation that the work is right and moving.

**EDITING — the handoff of authorship, made visible.** Sometimes the founder wants to change a word. They click into the draft. The field takes focus with a ring that *grows* into place, never blinks. And as the founder begins to type — the AI's spine along the edge of the surface **quietly recedes.** Authorship is changing hands, from the employee to the founder, and the interface shows that handoff rather than hiding it. A live character count shifts calmly toward its limit — warm, then warning — never a blinking alarm. When they save, the draft is theirs. If they'd asked to *regenerate* instead, HIRA would have warned them first that their edits were about to be discarded — because it respects the founder's work exactly as it asks the founder to respect its own.

**AUTONOMOUS REPLIES.** For a conversation the founder has entrusted to autonomy, the choreography is different, and the difference is the entire trust proposition. The draft does **not** stop at the gate. The working line **flows through** — the reply departs outward on its own, a brief settle marks it complete, and the item moves to *handled.* The founder *sees it happen* — full transparency, nothing hidden — but is not asked to act. They can review it after the fact, always. Watching autonomy *complete itself in the open* is what makes it trustworthy; an AI that acts invisibly is an AI you fear, and an AI that acts *visibly, correctly, without you* is an AI you promote.

**ESCALATION — the employee knowing its limits.** A message comes in that the receptionist is *not sure about* — an angry customer, an edge case, a question outside what it knows. And here HIRA does the thing that, paradoxically, builds the most trust of all: **it does not guess.** The working line **redirects upward** — toward the founder, toward the top of the frame — and the item *routes* to the founder's attention with a plain one-line reason. *"I wasn't confident about this one, so I'm bringing it to you."* Upward always means "to you." A tool that never escalates is a tool that will eventually send something terrible with total confidence. HIRA's willingness to *defer, appropriately, in the open* is precisely why the founder will feel safe letting it run.

**CAMERA: the founder, working through the inbox, unhurried, in rhythm.** Not fighting the tool. Not babysitting it. Working *with* it. Each action — approve, edit, entrust, receive an escalation — has felt like a small, clear, meaningful exchange with a competent colleague. Never a chore. Never a gamble.

## SCENE 6 — KNOWLEDGE

### *The receptionist becomes smarter — permanently, visibly.*

**INT. — KNOWLEDGE.** The founder decides to teach the receptionist properly. They paste their website's address to import it.

**THE IMPORT.** This is honest work and it takes a real moment, so we show real progress — never a spinner that lies, never a percentage bar that fills to ninety and stalls. The line works. As pages are read and understood, entries begin to **land** in the knowledge base — each one settling in, the count beside them ticking upward.

**KNOWLEDGE GROWS.** Ten entries. Thirty. Sixty. Each new fact **joins** the body of knowledge with the same "learning" gesture — a segment integrating into the whole, not a row abruptly appearing. The founder watches their business's knowledge become *the receptionist's* knowledge. Hours. Services. Prices. Policies. The name of the thing they do that customers always ask about.

**THE TRANSFORMATION — shown by return, not by claim.** Here is the direction that matters: we do not put up a banner saying *"Now 40% smarter!"* We never make a claim about intelligence. Instead, we let the founder *feel* the change by watching the receptionist come back to the inbox and **ground its next reply in something it just learned** — a chip landing that says *the exact policy from page three of their own website*, in an answer to a real customer, minutes later. The proof of learning is not a number in a knowledge screen. The proof is a better answer, grounded in a fact the founder recognizes as *their own words*, given to *their own customer.*

**THE FEELING.** *It's getting better.* Not "the AI improved." *My employee is learning my business.* And unlike a human hire, this learning is permanent, cumulative, and never forgets. Every conversation, every correction, every imported page compounds. The founder is not using a tool that stays the same — they are growing an asset that gets more valuable the longer they run it. That feeling — *compounding* — is one of the most powerful in the entire film, and we deliver it not by saying it, but by showing knowledge *join, integrate, and return* as a better answer.

## SCENE 7 — AUTONOMOUS MODE

### *Promoting an employee.*

**INT. — THE MOMENT OF TRUST.** Some days have passed in the edit of this film. The founder has watched the receptionist draft, ground, book, and escalate — correctly — dozens of times. They've stopped expanding the reasoning. They've noticed they approve almost everything unchanged. A quiet thought has formed: *I don't need to see every one of these before it goes out.*

They open the control. A single switch: **Assisted → Autonomous.** And the direction here is explicit and emotional: **this must feel like promoting someone.** Not toggling a setting. Promoting a person you've come to trust.

**THE WEIGHT OF THE MOMENT.** We do *not* make this a casual, frictionless flip, and we do *not* make it frightening. We give it *appropriate weight* — the weight of a real decision, made calmly. The founder is told, in plain words, exactly what is about to change: *"Maya will reply to customers on her own. You'll see everything she does, and you can step back in anytime."* No fine print. No alarm. No dramatic warning. Just an honest, clear statement of a real handoff of authority.

**THE GESTURE.** They confirm. And now the single most meaningful piece of motion in the product plays, once: **the review gate dissolves.** The stopping point where every draft used to come to rest and wait — it *opens.* Over a slow, deliberate beat, the barrier the founder has watched hold back every reply simply... releases. And the working line, which for days has flowed *up to a stop*, now **flows through.**

The switch's thumb slides over and settles with the smallest spring — the employee stepping into the new role. A single, quiet, resonant line of confirmation settles into place. *Maya is on her own now.* No fanfare. No celebration animation. A promotion is not confetti; it's a handshake and a nod.

**AND — CRUCIALLY — IT IS INSTANTLY REVERSIBLE.** The founder knows, without being told twice, that they can close the gate again in one motion, anytime. Trust that can't be withdrawn isn't trust — it's a trap. The founder gives autonomy freely *because* they can take it back freely. That reversibility, felt and never doubted, is what makes them comfortable letting go.

**CAMERA: hold on the now-open flow.** Drafts arriving and departing on their own, in the open, each one visibly completing. The founder watches a few. Then — and this is the shot the whole film has been building toward — **they look away.** They trust it enough to stop watching.

**CUT TO:**

## SCENE 8 — THE FOUNDER CLOSES THE LAPTOP

### *The last frame. The whole point.*

**INT. — EVENING, LATER.** The founder closes the laptop. A soft, physical motion. The screen goes dark. We hold on the closed laptop in a quiet room.

**CAMERA: slow, still, patient. We are not cutting away yet.**

Here is the emotional target, stated plainly, because everything in this document exists to produce this one feeling and nothing else:

> It is **not excitement.** Excitement fades by morning, and we are not building a thing you're excited about for a week.
>
> It is **not relief.** Relief implies you were drowning, and dread that it might not have worked. We are past that.
>
> It is **confidence.**

The founder closes the laptop and does not think about the business, and the *not thinking about it* is the product. Somewhere, a customer will message at 9pm. The receptionist will read them, check what it knows, answer them correctly, and book them for Saturday — grounded in the founder's own words, in the founder's own voice, without waking anyone. And in the morning the founder will open the laptop and find it *already done*, recorded honestly in a calm feed, exactly as they'd have done it themselves.

The feeling is the oldest feeling in good business, the one that made hiring worth it in the first place:

> **"My business kept running while I was away."**

Not because software processed some events. Because it feels like *someone was there.* Someone competent, calm, and trustworthy, who didn't need to be watched, who knew when to ask, and who got better every single day.

The founder didn't buy a tool.

They made a hire.

**FADE OUT.**

## HOW TO HOLD THIS FILM (for everyone building it)

This is the north star. Compare every decision against it. A few load-bearing principles pulled out of the scenes, so they're not lost in the prose:

- **Stillness is a feature, not a gap.** The idle receptionist (Scene 4), the unmoving KPI, the calm inbox — these are *deliberate.* When you're tempted to add "a little life" to a still state, remember Scene 4: the confidence of this product lives partly in how comfortable it is doing nothing. Resist the fidget.
- **Motion means something changed.** (Scenes 3, 4.) If you animate something that didn't change, you're crying wolf, and you spend the scarcity that lets *real* changes read at a glance. Guard that scarcity.
- **Evidence before conclusion. Always.** (Scene 2.) Grounding lands before the answer, every time, everywhere, for everyone — sighted or not. This single ordering is the difference between trust and suspicion. It is never optional.
- **Direction is language.** (Scenes 3, 5.) Outward = to the customer. Upward = to the founder. Consistency here lets the founder read HIRA's intent without labels. Never break it.
- **Never fake intelligence, never fake work.** (Scenes 2, 4, 6.) No staged thinking delays, no count-ups that lie, no progress bars that stall at 90, no "we improved!" banners. Show the real work; let intelligence be inferred, the way you infer it from a good colleague.
- **The gate is a promise; opening it is a ceremony.** (Scenes 3, 7.) The stopped line in Assisted mode *is* "nothing sends without you." The dissolving gate in Autonomous mode *is* a promotion. And it is always, visibly, reversible — trust that can't be withdrawn is a trap.
- **Celebrate quietly, or not at all.** (Scenes 3, 7, 8.) HIRA never congratulates itself. Success is as quiet as a competent person doing the obvious right thing. No confetti. The reward is the work being done.
- **Everything degrades to calm and to accessible.** Every moment in this film has a form that works in perfect stillness (reduced motion), in perfect silence (no sound), and narrated to a founder who cannot see the screen. The film is not the animation — the film is the *feeling of competence*, and that feeling must reach everyone.

If a future feature can be described in a way consistent with this film, build it. If it can't — if it fidgets, hypes, fakes, decorates, congratulates itself, or takes the wheel from the founder — then it isn't HIRA, no matter what the spec says.

**The founder should never feel like they're using software.**
**They should feel like they hired someone.**

*End of the Experience Film.*
